DI × BCG Circelligence + SAP

A competitive product analysis for Hilti Aftermarket Service (AMS): where a proposed decision layer called DI could sit alongside the tools Hilti already uses.

Research cut-off 5 November 2026Audience: AMS leadership, service and repair operations, spare parts, circularity, digital and technology
No DI wedge is proven. The strongest candidate is conditional.

Recovered-component-to-demand matching is the strongest conditional wedge, inside a broader proposition of case-level aftermarket decision intelligence. Reconciling each decision to its actual outcome is how DI could show whether it worked.

This is a publicly identifiable product gap, and a possible build-versus-buy case. It is not a claim that SAP, BCG or Hilti cannot build the same thing, and Hilti may already have equivalent logic that public sources do not show.

Summary

AMS means Hilti Aftermarket Service: repair and maintenance, spare parts, service-centre operations, returned-tool assessment, refurbishment, component recovery and reuse, and end-of-life disposition. Hilti already runs these. DI is assessed only as a possible decision layer on top of them.

Key findings

Circelligence measures

A strategic circularity tool: scores, material database, dashboards, scenario player. Hilti confirms a portfolio score and database. Scenario use at Hilti is not confirmed.

Hilti already operates the activities

Repair centres, automated criteria-guided part extraction with individual part testing, pool and donation routes for returned Fleet tools, recycling partners. These results are Hilti’s, not DI’s.

SAP is more than a system of record

It has a repair application (S/4HANA In-House Service), rules, workflow, agents, an AI platform and integration tooling. It documents the building blocks for every DI function except the domain content.

Generic features are weak differentiators

Agents, MCP, orchestration and governance overlap with current or announced SAP capabilities. They help DI operate but do not set it apart.

The Circelligence framework in detail

This is the framework as BCG and Circle Economy published it in November 2021 (“Introduction to the CIRCelligence indicators framework”, version 1.0, read in full). It is the most detailed public description of the methodology. The current software and Hilti’s deployment came later and may differ E2.

Provenance and maturity

Launched by BCG in February 2020 with Circle Economy’s help; BCG is co-author. On Circle Economy’s six-level maturity scale it sat at level 2, “tested within a limited set of applications”, in 2021. That rating predates Hilti’s deployment.

Scores

More than 60 indicators. Quantitative scores run 0 to 100%, on a mass basis and a value basis. Qualitative grades run A to F. The pairing separates coincidental from intentional circularity.

Data effort

A web survey of about 50 quantitative questions (up to 2,500 data points) and about 100 qualitative ones. Nearly 100 input materials and up to 10 sales markets. Gaps are filled with sector averages or proxies. Data stays with the company.

Delivery model

First application is always paired with a BCG project. Later use is by self-assessment, described in 2021 as a free online tool. Reduced scopes are possible. BCG also offers cost-benefit assessments to prioritise initiatives.

For each step the framework reads inflow (circular versus linear), slow flow (lengthening versus shortening use), outflow, and qualitative aspects. The headline indicator, percent circularity, is the average of all performance indicators.

Circelligence and Hilti’s use of it

Circelligence is a strategic circularity assessment and scenario tool. BCG’s product page describes it as “part evaluative, part circular economy framework”. Its documented scope is company, portfolio and value chain, not individual tools or parts.

Pillar (BCG product page)What it saysLevel
QuantitativeBaseline of circularity performance; pulls data from SAP systems across the value chain; Excel or interface entry also possibleCompany, value chain
QualitativeSurvey of cultural and organisational readinessOrganisation
VisibilityDashboard of circularity scoresCompany, portfolio
Scenario playerModels changes to processes and raw materials; supports business cases and prioritising initiativesStrategy

The page does not mention case-level repair, disposition, component eligibility, inventory, demand, approvals or writeback. Those are not identified as documented standard capabilities in the sources reviewed.

What Hilti publicly confirms E1

  • A circularity score, evaluated yearly across the product portfolio and used as a basis for Scope 3 CO2 goals (Hilti and SAP releases, 22 Nov 2023).
  • A materials database on SAP BTP with about 250,000 tons of material mapped (TechTarget, 5 Feb 2024).
  • Remanufacturing at 70 repair centres, with extensive testing before reuse; an automated, criteria-guided extraction process since 2021, and each reused part tested individually (Hilti 2022 report).
  • Additions to the SAP landscape so reused items keep an identity and link to their parent tool (TechTarget, 5 Feb 2024).

Scenario use is not confirmed. In 2023 BCG said its latest evolutions “lay the groundwork” for integrating scenario assessments. Whether Hilti uses the scenario player is unknown.

The published recovery figures do not reconcile

BCG’s page says Circelligence insights led to 120,000 recovered spare parts in 2022 and 90 tons saved. Hilti’s own sources give a different series. These are Hilti-reported operating results, not DI results.

SAP capabilities

SAP provides applications, rules, workflow, AI, agents and integration. For every DI-like function it documents a general building block. The sources reviewed do not identify a prebuilt route-ranking, component-eligibility or recovered-part-matching application. Hilti’s licences and deployments are unknown E7.

S/4HANA In-House Service (formerly In-House Repair) E3

Renamed with release 2025. A newer process version (“In-House Service 2.0”) arrived with S/4HANA 2023 FPS02, replacing older repair-specific transaction types. It covers in-house service objects, a precheck decision on follow-up steps, quotations, service orders, confirmations with spare-part consumption, billing and returns logistics. Third-party

Cognite SAP writeback

Writes from Cognite Data Fusion into S/4HANA over OData V2. The only supported entities are maintenance notifications and attachments. Service orders, in-house service objects, stock and component recovery are not listed. It needs Cognite as the source, so it is not a route for DI to write to SAP.

Third-party

WSO2 Sales Order connector

Open-source client for the S/4HANA Sales Order OData API: create, read, update, delete for orders, items, billing plans, schedule lines, partners, pricing and process flow. Relevant to spare-part sales, not to repair, stock or recovery decisions.

Wedge assessment

Strong would mean the DI HTML explicitly supports a valuable capability that is not identified as a standard feature in Circelligence or SAP. Conditional means value is possible but needs data, development, integration or validation. Weak means SAP or Circelligence already provides substantially equivalent standard functionality. No candidate is Strong.

Conditional

A. Case-level decision intelligence

Explicit in the HTML and not identified in Circelligence or SAP. Narrower than first thought: SAP has a precheck decision point, DI has no standalone repair-versus-replace function, and Hilti already runs an automated extraction process.

Conditional · strongest

B. Component-to-demand matching

Explicit in the HTML. SAP supports the underlying transactions but no matcher was identified. Highest uncertainty: Hilti may already match parts to demand.

Conditional

C. Multi-route optimisation

Seven routes ranked under constraints. No donation or loaner route in the HTML and no validated optimiser. Folds into A.

Weak

D. Governed decision-to-execution

Every building block is a current SAP capability. Only the domain-specific loop content is distinctive.

Conditional

E. Economic optimisation

Case economics differ from Circelligence’s initiative business cases, but the HTML’s economics are a structure, not a validated optimiser. Folds into A and C.

What DI could add beyond Hilti’s extraction and testing process (hypothesis). Nothing here shows Hilti lacks case-level logic. If it exists, DI adds nothing there. If it covers extraction only, DI could add a net-value comparison for the whole item before dismantling, a destination and demand for each extracted part, stated reasons when a route is blocked, and a comparison of outcome with prediction.

Positioning and moat

DI is a proposed domain decision layer for returned tools and recovered parts.

Using the facts held in SAP and other systems and the rules Hilti owns, it would rank eligible recovery routes and part allocations with stated economics and uncertainty, route the choice for approval into the system that executes it, and compare the outcome with the prediction. Circelligence measures circularity at portfolio level. SAP supplies the applications, rules, workflow and AI on which the transactions run, and on which a comparable layer could also be built.

Why the wording changed. An earlier version called SAP what “records and executes what happens”. That oversimplified SAP, which also offers a repair application, rules, workflow and agents. The distinction that survives scrutiny rests on documented domain content, not architecture layers. It holds only until Hilti shows equivalent logic in its repair-centre process, a custom BTP build or an SAP application it already runs.

Why a customer might buy rather than build

  • A worked domain model (routes, gates, demand, outcome) instead of a blank platform.
  • The decision, approval, handoff and reconciliation loop is already connected in one place.
  • Evidence snapshots and provenance are part of the design.
  • No BTP project capacity committed to a non-core build.

These are arguments for a buying decision. They do not show DI is better than a Hilti-built solution.

No moat is proven. A moat could come from reconciled outcome history, Hilti-tuned rules, part-level demand data and embedded workflows. Each accrues only with use, and the outcome history may belong largely to Hilti. The model-error improvement shown in the HTML is seeded.

What not to claim

  • That DI replaces or competes with Circelligence on scoring, scenarios or material flows.
  • That SAP or BCG cannot build this.
  • That agents, MCP, workflow, integration or governance differentiate DI against SAP.
  • That Hilti lacks case-level decision logic or part matching.
  • That DI is integrated with any Hilti or SAP system.
  • That any Hilti result is DI’s, or that any DI figure is measured.
